<h1>How can the world engage with China to strengthen global environmental governance?</h1>
  <p>Sun, Y., and Harlan, T., 2025. Dialogue Earth.</p>
  <p>12/18/2025</p>
  <p>Global environmental action is under serious threat, <a href="https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/01/putting-america-first-in-international-environmental-agreements/">driven</a> in part by Donald Trump’s renewed anti-climate agenda, including withdrawing the United States from the Paris Agreement.</p>
  <p>At the same time, escalating geopolitical tensions are <a href="https://dialogue.earth/en/climate/the-shape-of-a-new-climate-politics-emerges/">pulling</a> governments’ attention away from the climate crisis and toward security, trade and industrial competition. In this unsettled landscape, China is increasingly stepping in to a more influential role in global environmental governance.</p>
  <p>China, the world’s second largest economy, is a key player in multilateral institutions and the leading producer of clean technologies. As such it will inevitably be drawn in to the <a href="https://www.reuters.com/sustainability/climate-energy/can-china-fill-vacuum-climate-leadership--ecmii-2025-11-04/">vacuum</a> of climate and environmental leadership in some shape or form.</p>
  <p>But what might that look like? And what does it mean for the rest of the world?</p>
