From 7 – 10th September 2026, SGAIN members attended the Bath Conference on Earth System Governance (ESG). The annual ESG conference was centred around “Building just and sustainable futures for planetary integrity”, with SGAIN PI Yixian Sun chairing. Across the four-day event, SGAIN organised three sessions on China’s role in global green governance. 

In “China’s emerging leadership in global environmental governance and green soft power”, members presented our new conceptualisation of green soft power, alongside an introduction to the forthcoming book and the CGEL database. The session also saw breakout discussions, where SGAIN led conversations in regional groups to dig into the rise and effects of China’s green soft power in different contexts.

At “China’s ‘green’’ investments and sustainability transitions in the Global South” and “The rise of China’s global green soft power” on Wednesday, team members Yuen Gu and Jiuyang Li both chaired and presented their cutting-edge research: fieldwork on Indonesian community experiences in China-supported renewables, and norm co-production in China-Pakistan, a paper developed in collaboration with the Sustainable Development Policy Institute (SDPI). 

Across these two events, speakers brought together perspectives on Indonesia's ESG politics, global trust/distrust dynamics, the Green Belt and Road's energy transition impact in Bangladesh, desertification, China’s climate leadership and more. 
In the closing plenary, SGAIN doctoral researcher Muhammad Faisal Sharif moderated the panel on early career researcher’s reflections on the conference and the future of the field.

Finally, on Friday 11th September, our full international SGAIN team came together at the University of Bath for a full day of reflection, collaboration, and planning. 

Members from Bath, Indonesia, Bangladesh, China, and Pakistan reflected on the ESG conference, presented their ongoing and planned research, and finally shaped 2026/27 work plans in focused break-out sessions. 

Together, the conference and the follow-up planning day underscored the breadth and momentum of SGAIN's work on China's evolving role in global environmental governance. With new research directions agreed and international collaborations strengthened, the network heads into 2026/27 well-positioned to deepen this work and share its findings even more widely.
