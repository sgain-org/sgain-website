On 4 July 2026, we organised a workshop entitled "The Shifting Landscape of Global Climate Governance" in Shanghai. The workshop was co-convened by our PI, Professor Yixian Sun, and our China team lead, Professor Bowen Yu, at the School of International Relations and Public Affairs (SIRPA) of Fudan University. Bringing together around twenty leading experts on climate policy and governance from top Chinese universities, the Ministry of Ecology and Environment, the Ministry of Science and Technology, and China Meteorological Administration, the workshop provided a platform for transdisciplinary dialogue on how global climate governance is evolving in response to geopolitical change and the role that China can play in advancing climate action.

Discussions explored three interconnected themes shaping the future of climate governance. The first examined recent developments in multilateral climate governance, including changing negotiation processes, agenda-setting, and the growing role of science, AI and non-state actors in climate action. The second focused on the increasingly close links between climate change and other issues such as international development, litigation, and trade, reflecting the growing complexity of climate governance. The final session considered China's evolving role in global climate governance, exploring China's contribution through environmental diplomacy, export and investment of clean technologies, and South–South climate cooperation. SGAIN researchers made active contributions throughout the workshop. Dr. Yitong Ye presented the newest research on China's global environmental leadership and PhD researcher Yuen Gu shared findings on China's international climate finance and its implications for global climate governance.

The workshop concluded with an interactive breakout session on future research priorities and policy recommendations. Participants reflected on the declining political salience of climate change amid growing geopolitical competition and discussed how climate governance should be understood alongside broader transformations in the international order and political economy. They also highlighted the importance of communicating China's experiences to a global audience through clearer narratives and internationally accessible knowledge. The event further strengthened SGAIN's research network in China and created new opportunities for collaborative research, knowledge exchange and policy engagement. During the visit, Professor Yixian Sun also represented the University of Bath in signing a Memorandum of Understanding with Fudan University’s SIRPA, providing a foundation for continued collaboration between the two institutions.
